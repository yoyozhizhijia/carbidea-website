Which Grade Should I Choose?

Quick answers to the questions we hear most from engineers and buyers.

Which grade for maximum wear resistance?

YG6 or YG6X. Lower cobalt means higher hardness, so these grades resist abrasion best. Use YG6X (fine grain) where you need the highest hardness and a smooth wear surface. Expect lower toughness — avoid heavy impacts.

Which grade for impact and toughness?

YG15 or YG20. More cobalt binder absorbs impact energy and resists chipping. YG20 is the toughest of the standard grades — choose it for cold heading punches, stamping dies and parts that take repeated shocks. You trade some hardness for that toughness.

Which grade for high-temperature service?

Low-binder, fine-grain grades. Hardness retention at elevated temperature is better with lower cobalt content. If the part runs hot (hot forming tools, extrusion dies), a fine-grain low-binder grade is the right starting point — tell us the operating temperature and we'll confirm the grade.

Do you offer non-magnetic tungsten carbide?

Yes. Non-magnetic grades use a nickel-based binder instead of cobalt. They're used where magnetic interference matters — motor and sensor tooling, semiconductor fixtures, and parts near magnetic measurement equipment.

Do you offer corrosion-resistant carbide?

Yes. For chemical valves, petrochemical parts and aggressive media, nickel-binder grades resist binder leaching far better than standard cobalt grades. Application conditions (media, temperature, pressure) determine the right grade.
